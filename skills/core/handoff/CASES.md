# Handoff — judgment cases

Three handoffs that read as complete and were not. Each is generalised; each ends in the rule it bought.

## 1. The narrative that quietly excluded two-thirds

A handoff opened with a heading like "What got built this session" and ran through the session in the order it happened. The reader who picked it up next found, days in, a whole second workstream nobody had mentioned, a provisioned service nobody had said was running, and a known bug that had been live for a month. None of it was hidden on purpose — none of it had been touched in the session that wrote the document, so nothing in a "what I did" narrative asked the writer to go looking for it.

**Rule:** answer a fixed set of categories, not the story of one session. A checklist has edges a narrative doesn't, and `none` is an answer a narrative never produces.

## 2. The handoff that became a second decision log

A handoff restated, in prose, every recent entry from the project's decision log, on the theory that the reader shouldn't have to open two documents. Three weeks later a decision got amended; the log picked up the change, and the handoff — already read, already acted on — still said the old thing. The next reader trusted whichever one they'd opened first.

**Rule:** point at the log, don't retell it. A restated fact has no way to learn that the original changed.

## 3. The red line that lived nowhere because nobody had touched it that session

An account with a hard usage cap had been provisioned two sessions earlier and never came up again — until the handoff, organized around what had just happened, had no place for something nobody had just done. The next session ran a routine job against it and blew through the cap within the hour.

**Rule:** an asset earns a line by being live and consequential, not by being recently touched. The checklist asks what's already spent and what happens if it's misused, not what the writer did this session.
